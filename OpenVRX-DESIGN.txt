OpenVRX: PC-decoded analog 5.8 GHz FPV receiver
==============================================

Design and implementation context. Written 2026-10-19.
Owner: Julien (JulienChagnon). Status: idea and plan, nothing built yet.

This file is the context for implementing OpenVRX. It explains what the
system is, how every layer works, the order to build it in, and what is
still unknown. Read sections 1-3 first; the rest is reference.

Evidence labels used in this file (same spirit as C5VRX's AGENTS.md: an
idea is an idea, a simulation is a simulation, a board test is a board
test):

  [datasheet]   from a manufacturer datasheet or SDK header
  [zw-board]    measured on hardware by zerowidth in their C5 + P4 build
  [c5vrx]       from the C5VRX repo (code or docs)
  [sim]         simulation result
  [estimate]    reasoned estimate, not measured
  [idea]        proposal, nothing tested

MORE IDEAS: Getting the best resolution through a 20 MB/s pipe
Roughly in order of how much they help:

1. Send the angle, not I and Q. This is the biggest win. FM only needs the angle, so have the C5 convert each sample to an angle with a lookup table and send that as one byte. You get 256 angle steps (about 1.4° each), instead of the coarse grid that I4/Q4 gives you. It’s still PC-side demod: the C5 only converts coordinates, and all the real processing (differentiating, filtering, sync, deinterlacing) happens on the PC. The project’s existing lookup table already does nearly this; it would just need to output 8 bits instead of 6. The catch is that you lose signal strength, which diversity needs, though you can estimate quality another way (below).

2. Average instead of skipping. Instead of throwing away 3 of every 4 samples, add the 4 together. This acts as a crude anti-alias filter, so less noise folds into the picture, and averaging 4 samples gains about 1 bit of real precision. It depends on whether the BitScrambler or the CPU can keep up with the additions, and that has to be measured.

3. Use the bits you keep. When you cut 10 bits down to 4 or 5, choose which ones to keep based on the actual signal level. If the signal only uses the bottom half of the range, keeping the top bits wastes them. A simple automatic gain step, where the C5 adjusts a shift amount every so often, makes sure the signal fills the range you transmit.

4. A 16-bit bus at about 40 MB/s (I8/Q8, or a 16-bit angle) as a stretch goal. It’s at the FX2’s ceiling, so treat it as an experiment, not the plan.

Fancier PC-side processing
All of this becomes possible once the raw signal is on a PC.

Basic chain:

a channel filter before the discriminator, which cleans noise outside the band and pushes back the point where the picture breaks up,
a polar discriminator to get the video signal,
de-emphasis, if the VTX applies pre-emphasis,
a video lowpass at about 5 MHz.
Time-base correction. This means locking onto every horizontal sync pulse and resampling each line to exactly the right length. It removes wobble and jitter, and it gives the biggest visible quality jump after a clean demod.

Spark/click removal. When the signal is weak, FM produces white and black “sparkles” from impossible phase jumps. You can detect them and patch them from neighboring pixels or the same spot on the previous line.

Deinterlacing. PAL and NTSC send odd and even lines as separate half-frames. Your options:

bob: line-double each field; lowest latency,
weave: merge the two fields; sharp, but motion causes combing,
motion-adaptive (yadif-style): the best quality, at the cost of a frame or so of latency.
Color. The C5 path is monochrome only, but on the PC you can decode PAL/NTSC color. Expect 4-bit quantization noise to hurt color more than brightness, which is another reason to use the angle encoding.

Temporal denoise. Averaging across frames gives a cleaner image but adds lag, so make it optional for recording rather than flying.

Look at ld-decode / vhs-decode before writing all this from scratch. They decode FM-modulated composite video (from LaserDisc and VHS tapes) in software and already implement time-base correction, dropout fixing and color decoding. The problem is very similar to yours.

Line-by-line diversity
This is a strong idea, and a PC is the right place for it.

Hardware. Use two C5s, each with its own antenna (ideally different polarization or orientation), both tuned to the same channel. Two 20 MB/s streams total 40 MB/s, which is too tight for one FX2. Use two FX2 boards, ideally on separate USB controllers, or drop each stream to 4 bits per sample so both fit in one byte.

Alignment. The two receivers have slightly different clocks. Demodulate both, find the sync pulses, and resample both streams onto a shared line timeline. After time-base correction, line N from receiver A matches line N from B.

Scoring each line:

noise in the blanking interval and back porch, which should be flat, so any wiggle there is noise,
the sync pulse’s depth and sharpness,
the count of sparkle clicks,
the arrow’s length (signal strength), if you send I/Q rather than angle only.
Combining:

Select: take the better line from each pair. Simple and effective.
Weighted blend: mix the two lines in proportion to their quality scores. This is smoother and usually better when both lines are mediocre.
Segment-level: score short pieces within a line, to handle a dropout that hits only part of a line.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT OpenVRX IS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

One sentence: one or more ESP32-C5 boards each stream raw radio samples
(I/Q) through a ~C$4 FX2LP USB board into a laptop, which demodulates
the FM, decodes the analog video, combines the receivers (diversity),
cleans the picture and shows it on the laptop screen or the Fat Shark
HD3 goggles over HDMI.

    antenna -> C5 (tuner, I/Q out) -> FX2LP (USB 2.0 bridge) -> laptop
    antenna -> C5                  -> FX2LP                  -> laptop
                                                    laptop -> screen / HDMI -> HD3

Why do it:
- The C5's own demodulator (U85Q) has to fit inside the BitScrambler
  with 4-bit I/Q. A PC can run an exact or much heavier demodulator on
  7-8 bit I/Q. [c5vrx] [zw-board]
- Real combining diversity (line by line, later sample by sample or
  before demodulation), not just switching. Commercial modules
  (ImmersionRC RapidFire, TBS Fusion in FUSION mode) do combine, but
  their methods are not published.
- Signal-quality numbers per antenna, fast enough to steer a pan/tilt
  tracker with patch antennas, no GPS needed.
- Digital cleanup a goggle module cannot do: comb-filter colour decode,
  motion-adaptive deinterlacing, dropout repair, later ML repair.
- Raw recordings that can be decoded again later with better code.

What it is not:
- Not lower latency than analog goggles (<1 ms). Target is 10-30 ms
  added, glass to glass. [estimate]
- Not proven to beat a RapidFire or TBS Fusion on range. The C5 is a
  Wi-Fi chip read through a debug bus; its noise figure for this use is
  unmeasured. The bench test in section 9 answers this early.

Target hardware owned or planned:
- Laptop: Dell XPS 15 9530, i7-13700H (14 cores / 20 threads), 16 GB
  RAM, Intel Arc A370M discrete GPU (plus Iris Xe), Windows 11 Home.
  USB: Intel USB 3.1 xHCI, Intel USB 3.2 xHCI, USB4 host router.
  The 9530 has three USB-C ports (two Thunderbolt left, one USB-C
  right); one Thunderbolt port is suspected broken. Windows reported
  all USB/Thunderbolt controllers OK on 2026-10-19, so test the port
  physically (section 10).
- Goggles: Fat Shark Dominator HD3. Native 800x600 LCD, mini-HDMI input
  that accepts up to 720p, shown at 800x480 16:9 in HDMI mode.
  [retailer listings, HD3 manual]
- Receivers: Seeed XIAO ESP32-C5 (arriving). Recommended additions:
  Waveshare ESP32-C5-Zero boards (section 4).

2. HOW THE C5 PRODUCES RAW I/Q (the physics, briefly)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The quad's analog VTX frequency-modulates a 5.8 GHz carrier with the
composite video (CVBS) voltage. The C5's Wi-Fi radio mixes the antenna
signal down to baseband and digitises it as two numbers per sample:
I (in-phase) and Q (quadrature). The angle of the point (I, Q) turns at
a rate equal to the frequency offset, and that frequency is the video
voltage. FM demodulation = measure how fast the angle turns.

Inside the C5, the modem's diagnostic bus MODEM_DIAG carries those
samples [zw-board]:
- Q on bus bits 0-9, I on bits 10-19, 10-bit two's complement.
- The bus takes a new value about every 12.5 ns (80 MS/s), timed by the
  modem's own 80 MHz clock. With the 802.11p setting on, it may change
  at 40 MS/s instead (fits the observations, not checked directly).
- The top 8 bits of each are live (bits 9-2 and 19-12). zerowidth uses
  the top 7 of each on 14 pads.
- The GPIO matrix can route any bus bit straight to a pad. No CPU, no
  register in between: the pads show the bus as it changes.
- I carries a DC offset that grows with gain (about 20 of +/-127 at
  gain 64, about 32 at 68, 84-90 on an empty channel at gain 77). Q's
  is near 0. The PC must estimate and remove it.

The sample clock:
- The C5's PARLIO TX peripheral, running in loop mode, outputs a clock
  divided from the 240 MHz PLL (PARLIO_CLK_SRC_PLL_F240M, the default
  clock source on the C5 [datasheet: ESP-IDF v6.0.2 clk_tree_defs.h]).
- The modem's 80 MHz bus clock comes from the same PLL, so the sample
  clock is frequency-locked to the bus: each tick keeps exactly one of
  every N bus samples with no drift. [zw-board]
- Usable dividers keep N whole: 240 MHz / (3*N):
    /6  = 40.00 MHz  (N=2)
    /9  = 26.67 MHz  (N=3)
    /12 = 20.00 MHz  (N=4)
    /18 = 13.33 MHz  (N=6)   <- zerowidth's choice
- Nothing re-times the data to the clock, so the clock edge can land
  while the lanes are changing. That is the sampling-phase lottery
  (section 5.4).

PARLIO TX facts on the C5 [datasheet: ESP-IDF v6.0.2]:
- Up to 8 data lines, clock output pin, optional "valid" signal (which
  always occupies the MSB data line), loop transmission supported,
  TX clock gating supported.

3. THE FX2LP (CY7C68013A) AND THE BOARD TO BUY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 What the chip is

Cypress (now Infineon) EZ-USB FX2LP, CY7C68013A. Still in production.
Inside [datasheet]:
- USB 2.0 High-Speed transceiver and protocol engine (480 Mbit/s).
- An enhanced 8051 CPU at up to 48 MHz with 16 KB RAM. It only
  configures the chip and answers USB setup requests; it is not in the
  data path.
- 4 KB of endpoint FIFO memory (EP2/4/6/8), 512-byte packets,
  double/triple/quad buffered.
- A slave FIFO port: external logic presents 8 or 16 data bits and the
  chip latches them on its interface clock (IFCLK) straight into the
  FIFO. Full buffers become USB packets automatically (AUTOIN).
- 3.3 V I/O with 5 V tolerant inputs. Supply current no more than 85 mA
  in any mode (50 typ / 85 max with 8051 running on High-Speed USB).
- No flash. Firmware lives in RAM. On plug-in the chip enumerates as a
  bare Cypress device (VID:PID 04b4:8613 is the usual default
  [unverified, user reports]); the PC uploads firmware with vendor
  request 0xA0, releases the 8051 reset, and the chip disconnects and
  reconnects as the new device ("ReNumeration"). A small I2C EEPROM on
  the board can hold just an ID (small 24C02) or the whole firmware
  (24C128 or larger).

Same trick as C5VRX: the data moves through hardware (pins -> FIFO ->
USB) with no CPU touching each sample, like PARLIO + DMA + BitScrambler
in the C5.

3.2 Slave FIFO synchronous write timing, external IFCLK
[datasheet: CY7C68013A Rev AD, Table 24]

  IFCLK period         20.83 ns min, 200 ns max  (48 MHz down to 5 MHz)
  tSFD data setup      3.2 ns min before the IFCLK edge
  tFDH data hold       4.5 ns min after the IFCLK edge
  tSWR SLWR setup      12.1 ns min
  tWRH SLWR hold       3.6 ns min
  IFCLK rise/fall      900 ps max (as an output; keep the input clean)

The data must be steady for 3.2 + 4.5 = 7.7 ns around each edge. The
C5's bus holds each value about 12.5 ns at 80 MS/s, so the margin is
about 4.8 ns minus lane-to-lane skew and clock jitter. That is tight
but workable with phase calibration (section 5.4). With the 802.11p
setting (possibly 25 ns per value) it is comfortable. Data is latched
on every IFCLK edge while SLWR stays asserted, which is what continuous
streaming uses (Cypress KB: holding SLWR asserted gives one word per
IFCLK).

Pin mapping, 56-pin package [datasheet]:
  FD[7:0]   = PB0-PB7  (8-bit mode uses only these)
  FD[15:8]  = PD0-PD7  (16-bit mode, WORDWIDE=1)
  IFCLK     = IFCLK pin (input when IFCLKSRC=0)
  SLWR      = RDY1      SLRD = RDY0
  FLAGA     = CTL0      FLAGB = CTL1
  SLOE      = PA2       PKTEND = PA6
  FIFOADR0  = PA4       FIFOADR1 = PA5   (00 = EP2, 10 = EP6)
  SLCS#     = PA7 (optional, leave disabled)
Polarities of SLWR, SLRD, SLOE, PKTEND and the flags are programmable in
FIFOPINPOLAR. IFCLK can be inverted with IFCONFIG.IFCLKPOL.

3.3 USB throughput reality
- Theoretical bulk maximum on USB 2.0 High-Speed: 13 x 512 bytes per
  125 us microframe = about 53 MB/s.
- Reported sustained for one FX2LP: roughly 27-40 MB/s depending on host
  controller, transfer size and number of queued transfers. Two FX2s
  together about 41 MB/s on one link in one report. [Infineon forum]
- OpenVRX must measure this on the XPS before committing to a sample mode
  (Phase 1 step 1).

3.4 Is the board you found the right buy? Yes.

"EZ-USB FX2LP CY7C68013A USB Core Board Development Board USB Logic
Analyzer With I2C Serial SPI Interface Module Mini USB", C$3.87.
This is the common blue CY7C68013A-56 breakout ("core board"). It
normally has: the 56-pin chip, 24 MHz crystal, 3.3 V regulator, a small
I2C EEPROM with an address/enable jumper, a reset button, mini-USB, and
two long header rows with all port pins (PA, PB, PD, CTL, RDY, IFCLK,
CLKOUT). "Logic analyzer" means it works with sigrok; "I2C Serial SPI"
is listing filler.

Buy:
- 4 boards (2 for diversity, 1 for a third receiver or watch-mode
  tests, 1 spare). Plain "CY7C68013A" option. Skip any "+ Source Code"
  option: all needed software is free (libfx2, fx2lafw, Cypress
  examples).
- On arrival check: headers labelled IFCLK, PB0-7, PD0-7, RDY0/RDY1,
  PA4/PA5; the EEPROM jumper; the chip marking CY7C68013A-56PVXC (SSOP)
  or -56LTXC (QFN).
- Mini-USB cables: mini-USB to USB-C (direct to laptop) or mini-USB to
  USB-A (into a hub), short (0.3-0.5 m).
- Optional: one 24LC128 or 24LC256 EEPROM (about $1) per board if you
  later want the firmware to boot without the PC uploading it.

Alternatives considered and rejected for now:
- Raspberry Pi Pico / RP2350: USB full-speed only (12 Mbit/s), too slow.
- FT232H: High-Speed, but in its fast FIFO mode the FTDI chip generates
  the clock; the C5's bus cannot follow it.
- ESP32-P4 (zerowidth hardware): works, but 2 inputs max and much more
  firmware; good if a self-contained stick is wanted later.
- Cypress FX3 (USB 3, 32-bit GPIF II): the upgrade path for 4+
  receivers on one cable, but only with a shared clock (section 12).
- HackRF One (~$100-150 clone, ~$350 genuine): 8-bit I/Q at 20 MS/s,
  1 MHz-6 GHz, no hacking. Too expensive per receiver; useful as a
  "gold standard" reference receiver later.
- FPGA (Tang Nano 20K etc.): the right tool for many unsynchronised
  receivers into one link; larger project.

4. RECEIVER BOARDS AND WIRING
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Which C5 board

Seeed XIAO ESP32-C5 [Seeed wiki]:
- Header pins: D0=GPIO1, D1=GPIO0, D2=GPIO25, D3=GPIO7, D4=GPIO23,
  D5=GPIO24, D6=GPIO11, D7=GPIO12, D8=GPIO8, D9=GPIO9, D10=GPIO10.
- Back JTAG pads: MTMS=GPIO2, MTDI=GPIO3, MTCK=GPIO4, MTDO=GPIO5.
- Internal: GPIO6 battery ADC, GPIO26 battery-divider enable, GPIO27
  user LED, GPIO28 BOOT. USB (GPIO13/14) is not on pads.
- U.FL antenna connector (antenna included). 21 x 17.8 mm.
- Usable GPIOs: 11 header + 4 back pads = 15.
  -> fits 7 I + 7 Q + clock = 15 exactly, with the USB console kept.
  -> or 4 I + 4 Q + clock = 9 (8-bit mode), front pins only.
- Note: the back pads are needed for 7+7. Do not solder the XIAO flat to
  perfboard if it will be used for OpenVRX; use headers or wire the pads
  first.

Waveshare ESP32-C5-Zero (recommended second receiver type) [zw-board
plan, Waveshare schematic]:
- Edge pins 0-14, back pads 23, 24, 25, 28. GPIO13/14 are its USB.
  GPIO26 is internal antenna select (onboard antenna vs U.FL).
- Keeping USB for control: 0-12 (13 pins) + 23, 24, 25, 28 = 17 pins.
  -> fits 8 I + 8 Q + clock = 17: the full 8 live bits, 16-bit FX2 mode.
- Strapping pins 2, 7, 25 (and 28 with a 10k pull-up): safe as data
  outputs because the FX2 inputs are high-impedance while the C5 reads
  its straps at reset. [zw-board]
- GPIO0/1 are 32 kHz crystal pins with no crystal fitted: plain GPIOs.
- About $7-10 each [estimate]. 18 x 28 mm.

Recommendation: for the first diversity pair use two identical boards.
Either 2x C5-Zero (8+8, simplest pads) or the XIAO plus one more XIAO
(7+7). Mixed boards work but complicate comparisons.

4.2 Sample formats (lanes on the FX2 port)

Mode A (default for development): 16-bit, 8 I + 8 Q (C5-Zero) or
7 I + 7 Q (XIAO), clock 13.33 MHz.
  PB0-PB7 = Q bits (LSB..MSB), PD0-PD7 = I bits (LSB..MSB).
  With 7+7 the unused PB0/PD0 are tied to GND; the PC reads each lane
  as 2v+1 (append a constant 1 bit, centring the rounding, as zerowidth
  does by tying a PARLIO line to 1). [zw-board]
  Data rate 26.67 MB/s. One receiver per USB link.
Mode B: 8-bit, 4 I + 4 Q, 13.33 MHz. PB0-3 = Q, PB4-7 = I (same layout
  as C5VRX's PARLIO RX). 13.33 MB/s. Two receivers per USB link.
Mode C: 8-bit, 4 I + 4 Q, 20 MHz. 20 MB/s. One per link (+ watchers).
Mode D: 8-bit, 4 I + 4 Q, 26.67 MHz. 26.67 MB/s. One per link.
Watch mode (any of the above): the FX2 forwards 1 packet in N
  (section 6.3), e.g. N=16 -> 1.7 MB/s at 16-bit.

Which 4 bits in modes B-D: C5VRX uses ADC bits {9,6,5,4} (its
"ultrafine lane", accepting folding beyond +/-128 at its gain policy)
[c5vrx]. The PC can pick any 4 bits through the GPIO matrix; test both
top-4 and C5VRX's choice.

Trade-offs to settle by simulation first (section 9, experiment S1):
- 13.33 MS/s wraps the phase difference at +/-6.67 MHz. A transmitter
  that deviates -5.0 to +4.4 MHz is 1.7 MHz from wrapping at the sync
  tip; that cost about 2 dB of threshold against 26.67 MS/s.
  [zw-board, sim on captures]. Wrap repair (despeck) recovers much of
  it.
- So: 8+8 at 13.33 MS/s vs 4+4 at 26.67 MS/s, both 26.67 MB/s. Unknown
  which gives the better picture at the edge of range. Capture in
  Mode A first: dropping bits in software emulates 4+4, so one
  recording answers part of the question.

4.3 Wiring one receiver (C5 -> FX2 board)

  C5 lane pins (14 or 16)  -> FX2 PB0-7 / PD0-7 (or PB only in 8-bit)
  C5 PARLIO TX clock pin   -> FX2 IFCLK
  GND                      -> GND, at least two wires, one beside the
                              clock wire
  FX2 board 5V (USB VBUS)  -> C5 5V pin (optional: one-cable receiver;
                              see 4.4)
  FX2 SLWR (RDY1)          -> tie active permanently (default polarity
                              is active-low: tie to GND), or drive from
                              a C5 PARLIO TX data bit for gating
                              (section 6.3 option B)
  FX2 FIFOADR0/1 (PA4/PA5) -> GND (selects EP2)
  FX2 SLRD (RDY0), SLOE (PA2), PKTEND (PA6) -> inactive (3.3 V for
                              active-low defaults)

Practice [zw-board]:
- 30 AWG wire (Kynar wire-wrap), signal wires 3-5 cm. At these lengths
  wires are plain wires; length matching is irrelevant (2 cm ~ 0.1 ns).
- Keep each bundle with its ground alongside; minimise loop area.
- Low drive strength on the C5 data and clock pins; if errors, 22-33 ohm
  series resistors at the C5 end. Choose drive from the edge check
  (5.4), not by defaulting to the weakest.
- Short, direct, no breadboard for the final build (breadboard OK for
  first light at low risk).

4.4 Power and cables

- FX2 board: <=85 mA. C5 receiving: roughly 100-150 mA [zw estimate].
  Both fit one USB 2.0 port (500 mA). [estimate]
- v1 (simplest): two cables per receiver. The C5's own USB-C gives it
  power plus the control console; the FX2's mini-USB carries data.
  Do not also feed the C5 from the FX2's 5V in this mode.
- v2 (tidy): one cable. The FX2 board's 5V feeds the C5's 5V pin; C5
  control goes through the FX2: PC -> FX2 vendor request -> FX2 UART
  (the FX2LP has two UARTs) -> C5 UART RX. Needs 2 more C5 pins (TX/RX),
  which the C5-Zero has only in 7+7 mode; or use the C5's USB pins.
  Decide after v1 works.
- Common ground: all receivers end up grounded through the laptop's USB.
  Fine on a laptop. Do not mix a laptop charger ground and a separately
  powered tracker supply without a common ground point.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. C5 FIRMWARE ("radio head")
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Start from zerowidth's c5rx (the C5 side of P4USB) rather than from
C5VRX-5, because it already exports lanes and a clock. Credit both.
ESP-IDF v6.x (installed at D:\ESP-IDF\v6.0.2\esp-idf).

5.1 Responsibilities
- Tune: band/channel or raw MHz (R8 5917 MHz and above work via
  phy_set_freq [zw-board]).
- Gain: set a fixed gain index on command. The PC runs the gain loop.
- Export lanes: route MODEM_DIAG bits to pads through the GPIO matrix.
- Sample clock: PARLIO TX in loop mode, PLL_F240M source, divider from
  the PC (6/9/12/18), output on one pin.
- Clock slip: "clk slip US" raises the divider by one for about US
  microseconds, moving the edge against the bus by 240 MHz ticks
  (4.17 ns). The bus repeats every 3 ticks at 80 MS/s. [zw-board]
- 802.11p setting: phy_11p_set(1, 0) on every channel. Narrows the
  receive filter from about +/-9.5 to +/-6 MHz, lowered sync-tip noise
  on R8 (102-105 -> 87-91 kHz), and made the clock phase keep across
  retunes. Not understood beyond that. [zw-board]
- Lane self-test: a mode that drives the lane pins as plain GPIOs with a
  walking-ones pattern and a counter, so the PC can name any miswired
  or shorted lane ("Q5 reads on PB3").
- Status: firmware version, channel, gain, divider, 11p state, uptime.
- No DAC, no PARLIO RX, no BitScrambler, no video logic.

5.2 Control protocol (USB Serial/JTAG console, v1)
Line-based ASCII, one command per line, replies "OK ..." or "ERR ...".
Proposed:
  ver                     -> OK openvrx-c5 <version> board=<xiao|zero>
  tune <MHz>              -> OK tune 5732
  gain <index>            -> OK gain 64
  clk <divider>           -> OK clk 18 (13.33 MHz)
  clk slip <us>           -> OK slip
  lanes <map name>        -> OK lanes 8x8 | 7x7 | 4x4top | 4x4c5vrx
  selftest on|off         -> OK
  p11 on|off              -> OK
  status                  -> OK ch=5732 gain=64 clk=18 p11=1 up=123s
Keep the PC as the owner of all settings: on connect it pushes the full
state, so a C5 reset is harmless.

5.3 Timing of changes
A gain or tune change takes effect at some sample the PC does not know
exactly. The PC timestamps the command, then finds the step in the
stream (amplitude jump for gain, carrier jump for tune) and logs the
sample index. zerowidth found the I offset moves about half a unit per
gain step, so offset tracking must reset or jump on gain steps.

5.4 The sampling-phase lottery and how OpenVRX handles it
Problem: about 3 starts in 10 the clock edge lands while the lanes
change; some lanes hold the old bit, some the new. Near zero crossings
every upper bit flips at once, so mixed reads are garbage (Q read -19,
-113, 45 in a row). Cost about 18 dB of noise. [zw-board]
Fix in OpenVRX:
1. At start, PC asks for clk 6 (40 MS/s) briefly. The FX2 FIFO will
   overflow at 80 MB/s (16-bit), but a 2-4 KB burst is enough.
2. PC computes the probe: power passed by the fourth difference
   (1 -4 6 -4 1) over total power, each less its rounding share. Clean
   reads give near 0 (carrier 0.02-0.05, noise -0.2 to 6); mixed give
   19-80. Threshold 12. [zw-board]
3. If mixed: "clk slip 1", probe again, up to N tries. Three positions
   exist, one bad; one slip always leaves the bad one. [zw-board]
4. Then set the working divider. The phase position survives divider
   changes (14 of 14 positions read the same at 40 and 13.33 MS/s)
   [zw-board] and retunes with 11p on.
5. Re-check periodically on the live stream (glitch count far off the
   carrier ring) and after any C5 reset.
FX2-specific: the FX2 needs 7.7 ns of steady data per edge (3.2).
Whether one of the three positions always satisfies it at 80 MS/s is
unknown. If not, the 802.11p setting (if the bus really drops to 40 MS/s)
or IFCLKPOL inversion will not help at /18 (half a 75 ns period is
exactly 3 bus samples), so the fallback is fine tuning drive strength
and series resistors, then an oscilloscope look (the open zerowidth
item "look at the clock against a Q lane").

6. FX2 FIRMWARE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Toolchain: SDCC (8051 C compiler) + libfx2 (whitequark; chip support
package for FX2, with a Python "fx2" library and "fx2tool" to load and
debug; 0BSD license [package listing, confirm in repo]). Alternative:
fx2lib (LGPL). Use libfx2.

6.1 First light without custom firmware
sigrok's fx2lafw firmware + PulseView: samples 8 channels on the FX2's
internal clock (up to 24 MHz on 8-channel hardware). Use it once to
check the board, the driver, and that the lanes toggle. Not for real
capture: no external clock.

6.2 OpenVRX firmware, streaming mode
- IFCONFIG: IFCLKSRC=0 (external IFCLK), ASYNC=0 (synchronous),
  IFCFG=11 (slave FIFO). IFCLKPOL configurable by vendor request.
- EP2: IN, bulk, 512-byte packets, quad buffered (4 x 512 = 2 KB).
  EP2FIFOCFG: AUTOIN=1, WORDWIDE=1 for 16-bit or 0 for 8-bit.
  AUTOINLENH/L = 512.
- FIFOPINPOLAR: default (active-low SLWR) or active-high if driven.
- EP1 IN interrupt endpoint: status block every ~10 ms: firmware
  version, mode, packets sent, packets skipped, FIFO-full events
  (count of times the EP2 full flag was seen set while data was
  arriving = lost samples), IFCLK present flag.
- Vendor requests: start/stop, set width 8/16, set watch ratio N, set
  IFCLK polarity, read status, reset counters, UART relay (v2).
- Descriptors: own VID:PID. For a hobby project use a pid.codes test ID
  or apply for a free pid.codes PID; include Microsoft OS 2.0 (WCID)
  descriptors so Windows binds WinUSB automatically after renumeration.
- The blank chip (before firmware) still needs WinUSB bound once with
  Zadig so the PC can upload firmware.
- The PC host uploads firmware automatically on plug-in (vendor request
  0xA0 to RAM, then release CPUCS reset). No EEPROM programming needed.

6.3 Watch mode (sending less from a receiver)
Purpose: run 3+ receivers when only 2 get full streams (section 7.4).
Option A (preferred, FX2-only): switch EP2 from AUTOIN to manual
  (AUTOIN=0) and commit 1 packet in N, skipping the others (Infineon
  support describes switching to manual mode on a vendor command to skip
  packets). Each committed 512-byte packet is a contiguous burst: 256
  samples in 16-bit mode (19 us, about a third of a video line) or 512
  samples in 8-bit mode (38 us). The 8051 must handle about 52,000
  packets a second at 16-bit, 13.33 MS/s, which is a few hundred
  thousand simple instructions a second against about 12 MIPS
  available. [estimate] If it falls behind, the FIFO fills and samples
  are lost, which only matters inside a committed packet; check the
  full flag before committing.
Option B (C5 gating): a C5 PARLIO TX data bit drives SLWR with a
  pattern from a DMA buffer (all active = full mode; short bursts =
  watch mode). Clean timing because the bit is synchronous with the
  clock. Needs one more pin.
Option C (C5 slow clock): divide the clock way down (e.g. 1.33 MHz) in
  watch mode. Power and C/N estimates still work on slow samples;
  demodulated content does not. Keeps phase per zerowidth's finding.
Switching a receiver from watch to full takes a vendor request and one
USB round trip: about 1-2 ms. [estimate]

6.4 Throughput self-test
Before any C5 is wired: firmware option to use the internal 48 MHz
IFCLK (or 30 MHz) and stream whatever the floating port reads, with the
full-flag counter. Run 1, 2 and 3 boards on different ports and on one
hub, at 13.33/20/26.67 MB/s equivalents (set via divider of internal
clock or by commit ratio). Record MB/s and lost-sample counts.

7. PC HOST SOFTWARE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 Language and libraries (decision)
- Research and reference implementation: Python 3 + numpy + numba +
  scipy (C5VRX's research tools already use numpy/scipy/numba). Slow is
  fine; correctness and plots matter. Runs on recordings.
- Live engine: Rust.
  - USB: nusb (pure Rust; Windows via WinUSB, macOS, Linux; async bulk
    transfers; needs WinUSB bound, WCID descriptors recommended).
  - Display: wgpu (DX12 on Windows) or SDL2. Low-latency present mode
    (mailbox/immediate), fullscreen, max frame latency 1.
  - ML (later): ONNX Runtime with the OpenVINO execution provider (Intel
    discrete GPUs supported; Windows needs the OpenVINO package
    installed separately), or OpenVINO directly. DirectML is in
    maintenance mode; WinML is Microsoft's forward path.
  - Threads: crossbeam channels / SPSC ring buffers; pin hot threads;
    Windows MMCSS "Pro Audio" class for capture threads.
  Why Rust: memory-safe multithreading at tens of MB/s, good USB and GPU
  crates, single static exe. C++ (libusb + SDL2 + OpenVINO C++) is an
  equally valid alternative if preferred.
- Rule: every stage in Rust has a Python twin; golden test vectors
  (recorded or simulated .iq files with expected outputs) keep them in
  step.

7.2 Pipeline (per receiver, then combined)

  [USB capture thread]   one per FX2
     nusb bulk IN, 8-16 transfers queued, 16-64 KB each
     (16 KB at 26.67 MB/s = 0.6 ms of signal per transfer).
     Timestamp each completion (QueryPerformanceCounter).
     Push into a lock-free ring; count bytes; poll EP1 status.
         |
  [DSP thread]           one per receiver
     1. Unpack lanes -> signed I, Q (2v+1 for 7-bit). Lookup table on
        the 16-bit word is the fastest unpack.
     2. DC offset estimate and removal (I mainly; track per gain).
     3. Optional digital frequency shift (fine AFC, removes LO offset;
        two C5 crystals differ by tens of ppm = ~100 kHz at 5.8 GHz).
     4. FM discriminator: angle(z[n] * conj(z[n-1])). Exact atan2 or a
        large table (2^16 entries for 8+8 bits fits in L2 cache).
        Later: PLL / tracking demodulators for threshold extension
        (C5VRX's U85Q is a 32-phase tracker, kp=0.85 [c5vrx]).
     5. Wrap repair (zerowidth's despeck: a sample half a turn or more
        from the mean of its neighbours is wrapped; move it to the end
        of the range it wrapped past). Cut luma specks at C/N 7 dB from
        15 to 2.4 per thousand. [zw-board]
     6. Low-pass to video bandwidth (4.2 MHz NTSC luma + chroma band).
     7. Sync: slicer + line clock (PLL on hsync), field detection
        (NTSC: odd field broad pulse half-way through line 3, even
        field at line 4; wrap 263/262 lines [zw-board]). Track to
        ~0.1 sample. Resample each line onto a fixed grid: 4 x fsc
        (14.31818 MHz, 910 samples/line NTSC) is convenient for colour.
     8. Per-line quality: envelope C/N from |z|^2 (DC-removed
        mean^2/var, as C5VRX's cn_estimate: (c+1)^2/(2c+1)) [c5vrx];
        spike count; sync-tip noise; line-timing error. Also per-sample
        damage flags (envelope dips, wraps).
     Output: line records {rx, field, line, samples[910], quality,
     damage mask, timestamp}.
         |
  [Combiner thread]
     Align records from all receivers by (field number, line number).
     Same transmitter = same raster, so alignment is by sync, not by
     clocks. Buffer at most a few lines. Combining: see 7.4.
         |
  [Video thread]
     Colour decode: NTSC first (PAL later). Burst-locked subcarrier
     reference; 2D (line) comb v1, 3D (frame) comb v2 using the previous
     frame only (no added delay). At 13.33 MS/s the NTSC subcarrier is
     exactly 189/704 of the sample rate [zw-board]; on the 4fsc grid it
     is exactly 1/4.
     Field -> frame: bob v1; past-only motion-adaptive deinterlace v2
     (weave where still, interpolate where moving, compared against the
     same-parity field one frame back: no added delay).
     Denoise / dropout repair / ML (section 8).
         |
  [Display thread]
     Upload as a texture, draw, present immediately. OSD: per-receiver
     C/N bars, which receiver feeds each part of the picture, channel,
     latency counters, dropped-sample counters, REC indicator.
     Outputs: laptop screen, or HDMI to the HD3 at 1280x720 60 Hz via a
     USB-C to HDMI adapter (HD3 accepts up to 720p).
         |
  [Recorder] (any time, off the hot path)
     Raw stream per receiver + event log (section 7.6).

7.3 Control thread
- Opens each C5 console (USB serial), pushes state, runs the gain loop,
  AFC, phase calibration, channel scan, and (later) the tracker.
- Gain loop: aim the raw I/Q RMS at a target band (zerowidth uses RMS
  40-72 on 7-bit lanes; a 50 ms loop made a strong transmitter flash
  every second or two, fixed gain did not [zw-board]). Use a slow loop
  with hysteresis, change gain only during vertical blanking, and log
  each change with its sample index.
- AFC: measure the back-porch frequency; keep it near -1.0 MHz (C5VRX's
  porch target [c5vrx]) by digital shift, and retune the C5 only when
  the offset exceeds a threshold, during blanking.
- Channel scan: step channels, measure power and sync lock per channel
  (C5VRX's scan spends ~90 ms+ per channel [c5vrx]). With 2+ receivers,
  split the band between them to scan faster.

7.4 Diversity (the first goal)

Stage D1, selection per line:
  For each line, use the receiver with the better quality score, with
  hysteresis so it does not flicker between near-equal receivers.
  Already better than whole-picture switching.
Stage D2, weighted blend per line:
  Video = sum(w_k * video_k) / sum(w_k), w_k from each line's noise
  estimate (inverse noise variance, like maximal-ratio combining after
  detection). Smooth weights across lines.
Stage D3, per-sample repair:
  Where receiver A's damage mask flags a sample (spike, envelope dip)
  and B's does not, take B's sample. Removes most sparkles.
Stage D4, pre-detection combining (the long-range prize) [idea]:
  Combine the complex I/Q of both receivers before FM demodulation:
  estimate the phase difference phi(t) between them by averaging
  z_A * conj(z_B), rotate B by -phi, align B to A in time with a
  fractional-delay (Farrow) resampler driven by the line timing, then
  sum with weights. Coherent combining improves the C/N before the FM
  threshold, which is where analog video breaks up; up to ~3 dB for two
  equal branches in noise alone, more in fades [theory, estimate].
  Needs: per-receiver frequency correction (separate crystals), sample
  clock drift tracking (separate PLLs, tens of ppm), and robust phase
  tracking at low C/N.

Alignment notes:
- Receivers run on independent crystals, so their sample clocks drift.
  Each DSP thread maps its samples onto the transmitter's own raster
  (lines and fields), so D1-D3 need no clock relationship at all.
- D4 needs sub-sample alignment; the line clock gives ~0.1 sample.

Three or more receivers (watch mode):
- Up to 2 receivers stream full; the others stream in watch mode
  (1 packet in N). The PC estimates each watcher's power and C/N from
  its bursts.
- Promote a watcher when its quality beats the weaker full receiver by
  a margin for ~100 ms; demote at the same time; switch at the start
  of a field. [idea, numbers to tune]
- Works best when antennas point in different directions (slow-
  changing best direction); the two full streams handle fast fades.
- Open question (sim S2): 3 receivers at reduced bits vs 2 full + 1
  watching vs 2 full.

7.5 Latency budget [estimate]
  FX2 FIFO fill (512 B)                  ~0.02 ms
  USB transfer chunk (16 KB)             ~0.6 ms
  USB scheduling / completion            ~0.5-1 ms
  DSP per line + combiner line buffer    ~0.2-0.5 ms
  Field assembly (if display waits for a whole field)  up to 16.7 ms
  ML stage (if on, Arc A370M)            ~2-8 ms (unmeasured)
  Display (60 Hz panel, present immediately)  0-16.7 ms, avg ~8
  HD3 HDMI scaler                        unknown
  Total target: 10-30 ms added. Analog goggles add <1 ms.
Ways to cut it: present partial fields as lines arrive (beam-racing
style), high-refresh external monitor, skip ML when behind.
Measure, do not guess: build the latency tester (section 12, L1).

7.6 Recording format (.openvrx)
Per receiver, per session:
  <name>.rx<k>.bin   raw bytes exactly as received (lane words)
  <name>.json        sidecar: format version, board type, lane map,
                     bits, word width, divider and sample rate, channel,
                     gain log [{sample_index, gain}], tune log, slip
                     log, phase-probe results, USB transfer timestamps
                     (every Nth), FX2 status snapshots, laptop/firmware
                     versions, notes.
Replay mode feeds recordings through the same pipeline at real speed or
as fast as possible. sim.py output is written in the same format, so
development and tests run without hardware.
Data size: 26.67 MB/s = 1.6 GB per minute per receiver. Use the NVMe
SSD; record on demand (button) plus a rolling 30 s buffer in RAM.

7.7 Windows details
- WinUSB driver for the FX2 (auto via WCID after renumeration; Zadig
  once for the blank chip).
- Disable USB selective suspend in the power plan while running.
- Laptop on battery throttles the CPU/GPU; plug in, or in the field use
  a USB-C PD power bank (~100 W) through the hub's PD input.
- Port plan with two working ports:
    Port A: USB-C hub with PD passthrough: charger, HDMI to HD3,
            FX2 #1 (+ watch-mode receivers)
    Port B: FX2 #2 direct (its own USB 2.0 link)
  Rule: at most one Mode A (26.67 MB/s) receiver per USB 2.0 link; a
  hub is one link for all USB 2.0 devices on it, even a USB 3 hub.
  Check with USBTreeView which controller each device is on.

8. PICTURE CLEANUP (after diversity works)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Ordered cheapest first; all past-frames-only for live use.
  C1 Sparkle removal using the damage mask (no guessing from pixels).
  C2 Bad-line replacement from the previous same-parity field (what
     ld-decode / vhs-decode call dropout correction).
  C3 2D then 3D comb colour decode: removes rainbow and dot crawl.
  C4 Past-only motion-adaptive deinterlace.
  C5 Recursive temporal denoise, motion-gated.
  C6 Small ML repair network on the Arc A370M.
  C7 Offline heavy cleanup on recordings (bidirectional), like DVR
     Doctor (dvrdoctor.com, $4.99-7.99, which "rebuilds each frame from
     the frames around it").

Prior art to read: ld-decode and vhs-decode (software decoding of raw
RF captures of LaserDisc and VHS: demodulate, time-base correct,
dropout detect/correct with inter-field replacement, 2D/3D comb chroma
decoders). github.com/oyvindln/vhs-decode.

ML plan (C6) [idea]:
- Training pairs for free: extend C5VRX's research/sim.py (which models
  CVBS -> FM -> C5 channel filter -> noise at a set C/N -> sampling ->
  quantisation) to take real video frames as the picture, and emit
  noisy and clean outputs at many C/N levels. Add fading and multipath.
- Inputs: current field, previous cleaned frame, damage mask, per-line
  quality. Output: cleaned field. Small recurrent U-Net, int8 or fp16.
- Runtime: ONNX Runtime + OpenVINO EP on the Arc A370M. Budget: finish
  each field well inside 16.7 ms (aim < 8 ms); otherwise skip ML.
Safety rules (do not skip):
- Only modify pixels the damage mask flags.
- Stop filling an area after ~3 bad fields; show the raw noise again.
  Breakup is the pilot's range warning; hiding it is dangerous.
- Always show the per-receiver C/N bar while cleaning.
- One key toggles cleaned / raw.

10. TRACKER: PATCH ANTENNAS ON A PAN/TILT HEAD (no GPS)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Principle: amplitude-comparison monopulse.
- Two patch antennas on the head, pointed about +/-15-25 degrees apart
  (to be set by S4; patches have ~60-70 degree beams [estimate]).
- Each feeds its own C5 + FX2. They are also the diversity pair, so the
  tracker costs no extra receivers.
- Error = power_A(dB) - power_B(dB), using gain-normalised power: raw
  I/Q power minus each receiver's gain in dB (needs a gain-to-dB table
  per board, measured on the bench rig B3, and a swap test to cancel
  board differences).
- Zero error = the quad is on the boresight between the beams. Drive
  the pan servo to keep the error near zero.
- Tilt: patches are tall in coverage; tilt matters close in or high up.
  Add a second pair (or a 4-patch cross) for an elevation error later.
- Third receiver with an omni (watch mode) for reacquisition and close
  range, where the tracker should stop chasing.

Control loop [idea, numbers to tune]:
- Measure at line rate (15.7 kHz), average to 50-100 Hz.
- PI controller with deadband (+/-1 dB), slew-rate limit, and gain
  scheduling by signal level (noisy at low C/N, so slower).
- Lost signal (both below threshold for 0.5 s): hold, then a slow
  search sweep; reacquire with the omni's watch data.
- Pan beyond 180 degrees: classic 180 pan + 180 tilt "flip over" when
  the target crosses behind. Or a USB-rated slip ring later.
- Multipath (trees, buildings) can pull the head; the deadband and a
  plausibility limit on angular speed help.

Hardware [prices estimate]:
- Pan/tilt bracket + metal-gear servos ("MG996R pan tilt bracket"),
  ~$15-30. Bigger patches or helicals need stronger servos.
- Servo driver: Pololu Micro Maestro 6-channel (USB, documented
  serial protocol) ~$25, or a Raspberry Pi Pico (~$4) running a tiny
  USB-serial servo firmware.
- Servo power: separate 5-6 V BEC (~$5); never from a C5 or the FX2.
- 2x 5.8 GHz patch antennas, SMA ("5.8GHz patch antenna 8dBi SMA",
  Lumenier AXII Patch, TrueRC X-AIR) ~$15-40 each. Match polarisation to
  the VTX antenna (RHCP/LHCP).
- Mount the receivers ON the head with short U.FL-to-SMA pigtails to the
  patches; only USB cables (with a service loop) cross the moving joint.
  U.FL is fragile: use a bulkhead SMA and strain relief.
- Enclosure and patch mount: OpenSCAD, squint angle as a parameter.
Gain: an 8-9 dBi patch vs ~2 dBi omni is ~6 dB, about double the range
in free space. A 13-16 dBi helical gains more but needs good tracking.
[estimate]

13. RISKS AND OPEN QUESTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

R1 FX2 timing margin at 80 MS/s bus: needs 7.7 ns steady data per edge
   of a ~12.5 ns bus period. Mitigation: phase probe and slip, drive
   strength, series resistors, 802.11p setting. Check on hardware early
   (Phase 1.4).
R2 USB throughput per link may be below 26.67 MB/s on this laptop.
   Mitigation: Mode B/C, watch mode. Measured in B1 before wiring C5s.
R3 C5 sensitivity unknown; may be well behind a RapidFire/Fusion
   module. B3 answers it; LNA (H3) and D4 are the levers.
R4 The 802.11p setting is not understood (it writes modem and analog
   registers) [zw-board]. Treat as empirical.
R5 Gain changes move the I offset and power; the PC must track them
   per gain step or the demod and tracker error suffer.
R6 Laptop on battery throttles; field power needed.
R7 ML can hide breakup or invent detail; safety rules in section 8.
R8 Windows scheduling jitter on USB completions; mitigate with queued
   transfers, MMCSS priority, and buffering measured in lines.
R9 Two-cable receivers (v1) are messy; v2 single-cable needs the FX2
   UART relay and spare C5 pins.
R10 The broken Thunderbolt port: run the checks (power-drain reset,
   Dell SupportAssist / Dell Command Update for BIOS and Thunderbolt
   firmware, USBTreeView with a known-good device per port, F12
   diagnostics). Plan works with two ports either way.
